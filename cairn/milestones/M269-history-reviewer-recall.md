## Goal

The blame-history and prior-PR-comments reviewers run on the model that a planted-conflict recall test supports, Haiku 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5.

## Scope

**In:** a new note, `cairn/references/history-review-recall.md`, that plants known history conflicts in three local commits. Both models review each commit three times, and an Opus judge marks which planted conflicts each run caught. The note applies a verdict rule fixed before the runs. On `move`, the two reviewers move to Haiku in the skills, with a D-entry that supersedes D-151's history clause and a CHANGELOG entry. On `stay`, no skill or CHANGELOG line changes.

M228 and M264 (`haiku-sonnet-roles.md`, `haiku-sonnet-rerun.md`) counted reported findings that a judge rated valid: Haiku 12, Sonnet 13 over nine runs each, verdict `move`. Neither measured conflicts that a reviewer missed, which is D-016's reason ("can silently drop a real bug") and the reason D-151 kept both reviewers on Sonnet.

**Out:** cost and precision (invalid findings) are not re-measured. They rest on M264's figures, which the note and any D-entry cite. Search stays on Sonnet and stays in the candidate row "Haiku for the remaining Sonnet roles". Effort settings for the history reviewers stay unset (D-157), and no new effort measurement runs.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] AC1: The new note `cairn/references/history-review-recall.md` has a Design section with six parts. (1) Three planted-conflict tasks: two under the blame-history lens and one under the prior-PR-comments lens. Each task is a local commit, and the note names its SHA and its parent. At least one task edits skill prose and at least one edits code. (2) The patch of each task's two planted conflicts. Across the four blame-history plants, each of the lens's three forms occurs at least once: undoing a deliberate past addition, bringing back a fixed bug, and contradicting a D-entry. Of the two prior-PR-comments plants, one brings back a defect that an archived review recorded as fixed, and one contradicts a point that an archived review raised. (3) An answer key that names, for each plant, the commit, D-entry, or archived review finding that it conflicts with. (4) The reviewer prompt and the judge prompt. (5) The catch rule. A run catches a plant when one finding cites the plant's file and either a line inside the plant's changed hunk or the changed text, and also names the history that the key names or the milestone, commit, D-entry, or review that carries it. (6) The verdict rule. For each model, take each task's mean recall (plants caught divided by 2) over its three runs, and sum the means over the three tasks. The verdict is `move` when Sonnet's sum is above zero and Haiku's sum is at least 0.9 times Sonnet's sum. Otherwise the verdict is `stay`.
- [x] AC2: The note's Runs section has one row for each of the 18 reviewer runs (three tasks, two models, three runs each) and one row for each of the nine judge runs. Each reviewer row gives the agent id, the model id from the run's transcript, the run's count of numbered findings, and the judge's mark (caught or missed) for each of the task's two plants. Each judge row gives the judge's agent id and its model id from its transcript. The note states that the label mappings were read only after all nine judges returned. It also states the result of a search over the tool calls and tool results of all 27 transcripts for the note's path, this milestone file's path, and the key and mapping file names. The Verdict section applies the AC1 rule to the marks and states `move` or `stay`. If both models catch all 18 plant-runs, the Verdict section says that the test cannot show a gap on harder plants.
